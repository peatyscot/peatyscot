## Where the facts come from

Structured facts — founding dates, ownership, coordinates, official websites — come from
**Wikidata**, which is released under CC0 and may be reused directly. Every entity page
records its Wikidata identifier so any claim can be traced back.

Producer websites and regulatory texts such as the Scotch Whisky Regulations 2009 are used
directly for anything the producer or the law states plainly.

Wikipedia and published journalism are treated as **research leads only**. Prose here is
written from the underlying facts, not adapted from another writer's sentences.

Every page carries a **Sources** block with title, URL and the date the source was checked.

## How tasting notes are produced

Tasting notes on this site are **aggregated and labelled as such**. They synthesize
published producer notes and widely reported descriptions into a consensus profile.

They are not personal reviews. Nobody at peaty.scot claims to have tasted a bottle in order
to write about it here, and no first-person tasting experience is invented. Where a
description is contested, the disagreement is stated rather than resolved silently.

## Non-disclosure is recorded, not guessed

Producers frequently decline to state whether a whisky is chill-filtered or whether
colouring has been added. Where that is the case, the field reads **undisclosed**.

It would be easy to infer — a 43% Scotch is very likely chill-filtered — but an inference
presented as a fact is just a confident guess. The distinction between *known* and *likely*
is worth preserving.

## Corrections

Facts change: distilleries are sold, expressions are discontinued, strengths differ by
market. If something here is wrong, it should be corrected rather than defended. Each page
shows when it was last updated.
