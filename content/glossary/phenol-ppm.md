**Phenol ppm** (parts per million) measures the concentration of phenolic compounds in
**malted barley**, deposited there by [peat smoke]({{< relref "/flavours/peat-smoke" >}})
during kilning.

## What the number does not tell you

The measurement is taken on the malt, not the whisky. Between the kiln and the bottle,
most of that phenol content is lost — to fermentation, to the distiller's cut points, to
copper, and to years in oak. How much is lost varies enormously by distillery.

[Ardbeg]({{< relref "/distilleries/ardbeg" >}}) specifies roughly 55 ppm and
[Lagavulin]({{< relref "/distilleries/lagavulin" >}}) roughly 35 ppm, yet most drinkers
find Lagavulin the heavier and smokier of the two, because
[Lagavulin]({{< relref "/distilleries/lagavulin" >}})'s slow distillation carries more of
what it started with into a denser spirit.

Treat ppm as a specification of intent, not a prediction of experience. Where this site
records a ppm figure, it is the malt specification and is labelled as such.
