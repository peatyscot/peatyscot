**Phenol ppm** (parts per million) measures the concentration of phenolic compounds in
**malted barley**, deposited there by [peat smoke]({{< relref "/flavours/peat-smoke" >}})
during kilning.

## What the number does not tell you

The measurement is taken at the kiln, on the malt. Not on the whisky.

Between that moment and the bottle lies a long attrition. Fermentation takes some. The
distiller's cut points take more. Copper takes more still, and then years in oak take much
of what remains. Most of the phenol content measured in the malt never reaches the glass at
all — and how much survives the journey varies enormously from one distillery to the next.

[Ardbeg]({{< relref "/distilleries/ardbeg" >}}) specifies roughly 55 ppm.
[Lagavulin]({{< relref "/distilleries/lagavulin" >}}) roughly 35 ppm. And yet most drinkers
find Lagavulin the heavier and smokier of the two, because
[Lagavulin]({{< relref "/distilleries/lagavulin" >}})'s slow distillation carries more of
what it started with into a denser spirit.

Treat ppm as a specification of intent, not a prediction of experience. Where this site
records a ppm figure, it is the malt specification, and it is labelled as such.
