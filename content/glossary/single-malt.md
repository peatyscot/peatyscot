**Single malt** means whisky made at **one distillery**, from **malted barley only**, using
pot stills.

## What "single" refers to

Not a single cask. Not a single batch. A single **distillery**.

A standard single malt is a vatting of many casks — sometimes hundreds of them — of
different ages and different wood, every one distilled in the same place.

This is the term's most common misreading. Whisky drawn from one cask is a **single cask**
bottling, which is a different and far rarer thing.

## What it does not guarantee

Nothing about quality. Nothing about age, beyond the legal minimum for its country. In
[Scotland]({{< relref "/countries/scotland" >}}) that minimum is three years, and an age
statement, where one is present, refers to the **youngest** whisky in the vatting.

Nor does it guarantee a style.
[Lagavulin]({{< relref "/distilleries/lagavulin" >}}) and
[The Macallan]({{< relref "/distilleries/macallan" >}}) are both single malts, and they share
no meaningful flavour ground whatsoever — one heavily peated on
[Islay]({{< relref "/regions/islay" >}}), the other unpeated and sherried in
[Speyside]({{< relref "/regions/speyside" >}}).

Nor does it guarantee strength, or treatment. Single malts run from the 40% legal floor —
[Macallan Double Cask 12]({{< relref "/whiskies/macallan-double-cask-12-year-old" >}}) — up to
[cask strength]({{< relref "/glossary/cask-strength" >}}) bottlings well above 60%.
