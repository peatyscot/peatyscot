**Single malt** means whisky made at **one distillery**, from **malted barley only**, using
pot stills.

## What "single" refers to

Not a single cask, and not a single batch. It refers to a single **distillery**. A standard
single malt is a vatting of many casks — sometimes hundreds — of different ages and cask
types, all distilled at the same place.

This is the term's most common misreading. A whisky from one cask is a **single cask**
bottling, which is a different and much rarer thing.

## What it does not guarantee

Nothing about quality, and nothing about age beyond the legal minimum for its country. In
[Scotland]({{< relref "/countries/scotland" >}}) that minimum is three years; an age
statement, where present, refers to the **youngest** whisky in the vatting.

Nor does it guarantee a style. [Lagavulin]({{< relref "/distilleries/lagavulin" >}}) and
[The Macallan]({{< relref "/distilleries/macallan" >}}) are both single malts and share no
meaningful flavour ground — one heavily peated on [Islay]({{< relref "/regions/islay" >}}), the other
unpeated and sherried in [Speyside]({{< relref "/regions/speyside" >}}).

Nor does it guarantee strength or treatment. Single malts range from the 40% legal floor —
[Macallan Double Cask 12]({{< relref "/whiskies/macallan-double-cask-12-year-old" >}}) — to
[cask strength]({{< relref "/glossary/cask-strength" >}}) bottlings well above 60%.
