Scotch whisky is among the most tightly defined spirits on earth. The Scotch Whisky
Regulations 2009 require that it be produced at a distillery in Scotland from water and
malted barley, matured in oak casks of no more than 700 litres for **at least three years**,
and bottled at no less than **40% ABV**. Nothing may be added except water and plain caramel
colouring.

That last clause is worth pausing on.

Caramel colouring — E150a — is permitted, and it is widely used. The colour of a whisky
therefore tells you very nearly nothing about the cask that made it. A deep mahogany
whisky, presented as evidence of long years in sherry wood, may have arrived at that shade
at the very last moment before bottling.

## The five regions

The same regulations protect five regional indications: **Highland**, **Lowland**,
**Speyside**, **Campbeltown** and **Islay**. These are geographic boundaries. They are not
flavour guarantees. [Islay]({{< relref "/regions/islay" >}}) comes closest to implying a
house style, and even there the correlation breaks down — Bruichladdich and Bunnahabhain
both sit on Islay, and both release largely unpeated whisky.

[Speyside]({{< relref "/regions/speyside" >}}) holds by far the greatest concentration of
distilleries. The [Highland]({{< relref "/regions/highland" >}}) region is geographically the
largest, and the least stylistically coherent of the five.

## Malt, grain and blends

Most Scotch sold anywhere is **blended** — malt whisky married with lighter grain whisky
from column stills. A [single malt]({{< relref "/glossary/single-malt" >}}) is the product of
one distillery, from malted barley alone, and the word "single" refers to that distillery
rather than to a single cask.

Strength is the other axis the label understates. The 40% legal floor and a
[cask strength]({{< relref "/glossary/cask-strength" >}}) bottling are the same category of
drink at very different intensities.

Treat region as a place to begin, and
[flavour]({{< relref "/flavours/peat-smoke" >}}) as the thing you actually navigate by. If it
was smoke that brought you here, start with
