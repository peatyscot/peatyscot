Islay is a small island off Scotland's west coast with an outsized reputation, built on a
flavour most beginners describe as smoke and most enthusiasts eventually describe as
seaweed, tar, iodine and bonfire.

## Why Islay peat tastes different

Peat is compressed vegetation, and what grew in the bog determines what the smoke tastes
like. Inland Highland peat is heavy in wood and heather. Islay's peat formed in coastal
bogs from sphagnum moss, seaweed and salt-laden vegetation, and it burns to a smoke high in
the phenolic compounds that read as **medicinal** rather than **woody** — the TCP,
antiseptic and iodine notes that define the island's style.

The measurement of that intensity is [phenol ppm]({{< relref "/glossary/phenol-ppm" >}}),
and it is quoted for the malted barley rather than the whisky in the glass.

## Islay is not uniformly peaty

The reputation obscures the range. [Lagavulin]({{< relref "/distilleries/lagavulin" >}})
and [Ardbeg]({{< relref "/distilleries/ardbeg" >}}) sit within two miles of each other on
the island's south coast and both distil heavily peated spirit, yet taste markedly
different — Lagavulin denser and more sherried, Ardbeg brighter and more coastal.

Meanwhile Bruichladdich and Bunnahabhain built their identities on **unpeated** Islay
spirit. An Islay label is a postcode, not a promise.
