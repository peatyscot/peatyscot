Hokkaido is Japan's northernmost main island, and its whisky story begins with a man
looking for somewhere that reminded him of Scotland.

Masataka Taketsuru had studied chemistry in Glasgow and worked in Scottish distilleries.
When the time came to choose a site of his own, he chose Yoichi, on Hokkaido's west coast:
cool, damp, sea-facing, surrounded by peat bogs and clean water. About as close to the
Scottish climate as Japan offers.

Unlike the five [Scotch]({{< relref "/countries/scotland" >}}) regions, Hokkaido carries no
protected status whatsoever. Japanese whisky labelling regulates **where the whisky is
made**, not which part of Japan it comes from — see
[Japanese whisky]({{< relref "/countries/japan" >}}) for the 2021 standards.

Hokkaido does have peat, and Japanese distillers have used it. But the
[peat smoke]({{< relref "/flavours/peat-smoke" >}}) it yields reads drier and more mineral
than [Islay]({{< relref "/regions/islay" >}})'s maritime style. These bogs simply did not
form from seaweed. The measurement conventions are the same either way; see
[phenol ppm]({{< relref "/glossary/phenol-ppm" >}}).

The island's defining distillery is [Yoichi]({{< relref "/distilleries/yoichi" >}}), where
the stills are still fired over direct coal — a method long since abandoned in
[Islay]({{< relref "/regions/islay" >}}), and in the rest of Scotland with it.
