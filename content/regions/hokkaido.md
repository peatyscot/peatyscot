Hokkaido is Japan's northernmost main island, and its whisky story begins with a deliberate
comparison to Scotland.

Masataka Taketsuru had studied chemistry in Glasgow and worked in Scottish distilleries. When
he came to choose a site for his own distillery, he chose Yoichi on Hokkaido's west coast:
cool, damp, sea-facing, surrounded by peat bogs and clean water, and about as close to the
Scottish climate as Japan offers.

Unlike the five [Scotch]({{< relref "/countries/scotland" >}}) regions, Hokkaido carries no
protected status. Japanese whisky labelling regulates **where the whisky is made**, not which
part of Japan it comes from — see [Japanese whisky]({{< relref "/countries/japan" >}}) for the
2021 standards.

Hokkaido does have peat, and Japanese distillers have used it, but the resulting
[peat smoke]({{< relref "/flavours/peat-smoke" >}}) reads drier and more mineral than
[Islay]({{< relref "/regions/islay" >}})'s maritime style — the bogs simply did not form from
seaweed. The measurement conventions are the same either way; see
[phenol ppm]({{< relref "/glossary/phenol-ppm" >}}).

The island's defining distillery is [Yoichi]({{< relref "/distilleries/yoichi" >}}), still
distilling over direct coal fires — a method long abandoned in
[Islay]({{< relref "/regions/islay" >}}) and the rest of Scotland.
