Speyside follows the River Spey through Moray in Scotland's north-east and holds roughly
half of all Scotch malt distilleries — the densest concentration of whisky production
anywhere in the world.

## The house style

Where [Islay]({{< relref "/regions/islay" >}}) is defined by what it burns, Speyside is
defined by what it fills. The regional style is largely **unpeated**, letting cask
influence dominate: orchard fruit and honey from ex-bourbon wood, dried fruit and spice
from [sherry-seasoned]({{< relref "/flavours/sherry" >}}) oak.

[The Macallan]({{< relref "/distilleries/macallan" >}}) built an entire luxury positioning
on sherry-cask maturation, and its cask programme is a larger share of the finished
product's cost than the distillation itself.

The regional style is also where the [vanilla]({{< relref "/flavours/vanilla" >}}) signature of
ex-bourbon American oak is most audible, precisely because there is no smoke competing with
it. [Macallan Double Cask 12]({{< relref "/whiskies/macallan-double-cask-12-year-old" >}}) sets that
American oak against European in a single bottling.

The absence of smoke is a stylistic choice rather than a rule. Several Speyside distilleries
release peated expressions, and the region's historical whiskies were considerably smokier
than today's — peat was simply the available fuel for drying malt before coal reached the
glens.
