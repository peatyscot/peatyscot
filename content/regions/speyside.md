Speyside follows the River Spey through Moray in Scotland's north-east, and holds roughly
half of all Scotch malt distilleries — the densest concentration of whisky production
anywhere in the world.

## The house style

Where [Islay]({{< relref "/regions/islay" >}}) is defined by what it burns, Speyside is
defined by what it fills.

The regional style is largely **unpeated**, and that absence is the whole design. With no
smoke in the way, the cask is left to do the talking: orchard fruit and honey out of
ex-bourbon wood, dried fruit and spice out of
[sherry-seasoned]({{< relref "/flavours/sherry" >}}) oak.

[The Macallan]({{< relref "/distilleries/macallan" >}}) built an entire luxury positioning
on that principle. Its cask programme costs more than the distillation does.

It is here, too, that the [vanilla]({{< relref "/flavours/vanilla" >}}) signature of American
oak is most audible — precisely because nothing is competing with it.
[Macallan Double Cask 12]({{< relref "/whiskies/macallan-double-cask-12-year-old" >}}) sets
that American oak against European in a single bottling.

The absence of smoke is a choice, though, not a rule. Several Speyside distilleries release
peated expressions. And the region's historical whiskies were considerably smokier than
today's, for the simple reason that peat was the fuel to hand for drying malt, long before
coal ever reached the glens.
