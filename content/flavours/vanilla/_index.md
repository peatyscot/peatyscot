Vanilla is the most widespread flavour in whisky, and it comes from wood rather than grain.

American oak (*Quercus alba*) is rich in **vanillin**, and charring the cask converts oak
lignin into more of it. Since bourbon must by law mature in new charred American oak, the
American whiskey industry produces a continuous supply of once-used casks — which is why so
much Scotch matures in ex-bourbon wood and why so much Scotch tastes of vanilla.

American oak also gives lactones, which read as coconut and fresh sawn timber.

Prominent in [Macallan Double Cask 12]({{< relref "/whiskies/macallan-double-cask-12-year-old" >}}),
where American oak is set against European, and underneath the smoke in
[Ardbeg 10 Year Old]({{< relref "/whiskies/ardbeg-10-year-old" >}}).
