Vanilla is the most widespread flavour in whisky, and it comes from the wood rather than
from the grain.

American oak — *Quercus alba* — is rich in **vanillin**, and charring the cask converts more
of the oak's lignin into it. Bourbon must by law mature in new charred American oak, and can
use each cask only once. And so the American whiskey industry produces, continuously, an
enormous supply of once-used casks with nowhere else to go. That is why so much Scotch
matures in ex-bourbon wood, and why so much Scotch tastes of vanilla.

The same oak gives lactones too, which read as coconut and fresh sawn timber.

Prominent in
[Macallan Double Cask 12]({{< relref "/whiskies/macallan-double-cask-12-year-old" >}}), where
American oak is set against European, and underneath the smoke in
[Ardbeg 10 Year Old]({{< relref "/whiskies/ardbeg-10-year-old" >}}).
