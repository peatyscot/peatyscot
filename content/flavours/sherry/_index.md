Sherry character is cask-derived: dried fruit, raisin, fig, orange peel, clove, and a tannic
grip that ex-bourbon wood does not supply.

## Seasoned, not used

Almost no whisky today matures in a cask that genuinely held sherry for sale. The sherry
industry shrank, and the supply of authentically used casks shrank with it.

So the casks are made to order. New oak is filled with sherry — often made specifically for
the purpose — held for a period of years, then emptied and shipped to the distillery. This
is what **sherry-seasoned** means, and
[The Macallan]({{< relref "/distilleries/macallan" >}}) runs one of the largest such
programmes anywhere.

## The oak matters more than the wine

European oak gives tannin, spice and structure. American oak gives
[vanilla]({{< relref "/flavours/vanilla" >}}), coconut and a softer sweetness.
[Macallan Double Cask 12]({{< relref "/whiskies/macallan-double-cask-12-year-old" >}}) uses
both, sherry-seasoned, and the contrast between them is the entire design of the bottling.

Sherry appears underneath smoke as well, as it does in
[Lagavulin 16 Year Old]({{< relref "/whiskies/lagavulin-16-year-old" >}}).
