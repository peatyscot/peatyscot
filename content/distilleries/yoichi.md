Yoichi sits on the coast of [Hokkaido]({{< relref "/regions/hokkaido" >}}), Japan's
northernmost main island. Masataka Taketsuru founded it in **1934**, after studying
chemistry in Glasgow and working in Scottish distilleries; he chose the site because its
cold, damp, sea-facing climate reminded him of Scotland. It is the home distillery of
**Nikka Whisky**.

## What makes it taste like that

Yoichi's signature is a method now almost extinct in the country that taught it:
**direct coal firing**.

The stills are not warmed by internal steam coils. They are heated by coal, shovelled by
hand, reaching temperatures that run higher and less evenly than any coil would allow. In
that uneven heat, sugars and amino acids react more aggressively than they otherwise would,
and the spirit takes on a roasted, faintly scorched depth that steam heating simply does not
produce.

The distillery uses peated malt as well, though the smoke is not
[Islay]({{< relref "/regions/islay" >}})'s. The
[peat smoke]({{< relref "/flavours/peat-smoke" >}}) here reads drier and more mineral, and it
sits alongside the coal-fired weight rather than bringing the maritime
[iodine]({{< relref "/flavours/iodine" >}}) of the Scottish islands with it. The mechanism is
the same everywhere — see
[what makes a whisky peaty]({{< relref "/guides/what-makes-a-whisky-peaty" >}}).

## Core bottling

[Yoichi Single Malt]({{< relref "/whiskies/yoichi-single-malt" >}}) is the current
no-age-statement flagship, introduced after Nikka withdrew its aged Yoichi range in 2015,
when the stocks could no longer support it — a consequence of the demand surge described on
the [Japanese whisky]({{< relref "/countries/japan" >}}) page.
