Lagavulin sits in a shallow bay on the south coast of [Islay]({{< relref "/regions/islay" >}}),
between [Ardbeg]({{< relref "/distilleries/ardbeg" >}}) and Laphroaig. It was established
in **1816** and is owned by **Diageo**, where it anchors the Classic Malts range.

## What makes it taste like that

Lagavulin's defining production choice is time. Its spirit stills are run remarkably slowly
— a distillation regime substantially longer than the Islay norm — which increases copper
contact and produces a heavier, oilier, more rounded spirit than its neighbours achieve
from comparably peated malt.

The malt is specified at roughly **35 ppm** phenols, heavy by any standard but below
[Ardbeg]({{< relref "/distilleries/ardbeg" >}})'s specification. The finished whisky
nonetheless reads as denser and sweeter, because that slow distillation and a significant
proportion of [sherry-seasoned]({{< relref "/flavours/sherry" >}}) European oak in the fill
policy carry weight that lighter spirit could not.

The result is the least aggressive of the heavily peated Islays:
[peat smoke]({{< relref "/flavours/peat-smoke" >}}) as depth rather than attack.

## Core bottling

The distillery's flagship is
[Lagavulin 16 Year Old]({{< relref "/whiskies/lagavulin-16-year-old" >}}), one of the few
heavily peated whiskies to carry a mainstream age statement that long.
