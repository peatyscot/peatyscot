Lagavulin sits in a shallow bay on the south coast of
[Islay]({{< relref "/regions/islay" >}}), between
[Ardbeg]({{< relref "/distilleries/ardbeg" >}}) and Laphroaig. It was established in
**1816**, and it is owned by **Diageo**, where it anchors the Classic Malts range.

## What makes it taste like that

The defining choice here is time.

Lagavulin's spirit stills are run remarkably slowly — a distillation substantially longer
than the Islay norm. Vapour that rises gently spends far longer in contact with copper, and
what finally comes off is heavier, oilier and more rounded than the distillery's neighbours
achieve from comparably peated malt.

The malt is specified at roughly **35 ppm** phenols. Heavy by any standard, and still below
[Ardbeg]({{< relref "/distilleries/ardbeg" >}})'s figure, two miles to the east. And yet the
finished whisky is the denser and the sweeter of the two — because that slow distillation,
and a significant proportion of
[sherry-seasoned]({{< relref "/flavours/sherry" >}}) European oak in the fill policy, carry
a weight lighter spirit could not.

The result is the least aggressive of the heavily peated Islays:
[peat smoke]({{< relref "/flavours/peat-smoke" >}}) as depth rather than as attack.

## Core bottling

The flagship is
[Lagavulin 16 Year Old]({{< relref "/whiskies/lagavulin-16-year-old" >}}), one of very few
heavily peated whiskies to carry a mainstream age statement that long.
