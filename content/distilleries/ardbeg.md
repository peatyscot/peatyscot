Ardbeg is the easternmost of the three south-coast [Islay]({{< relref "/regions/islay" >}})
distilleries, a short walk from [Lagavulin]({{< relref "/distilleries/lagavulin" >}}). It is
owned by **LVMH** through Moët Hennessy, which acquired it via Glenmorangie in 2004.

**A note on the founding date.** Ardbeg is conventionally dated to **1815**, when the site
was licensed as a commercial distillery. Wikidata records **1794**, reflecting documented
distilling activity on the site before licensing. Both dates are defensible; they answer
different questions.

## What makes it taste like that

Ardbeg specifies its malt at around **55 ppm** phenols, among the highest of any regularly
produced Scotch. That alone would predict a brutal whisky, and Ardbeg is not one.

The moderating factor is the **purifier** on the spirit still — a device that returns
heavier vapours to the still for redistillation, stripping out oilier compounds and
producing a cleaner, more precise spirit. The peat arrives, but it arrives with citrus and
sea air rather than the weight that [Lagavulin]({{< relref "/distilleries/lagavulin" >}})
carries.

Ardbeg's fill policy leans heavily on ex-bourbon American oak rather than
[sherry]({{< relref "/flavours/sherry" >}}), which reinforces that brightness — ex-bourbon wood
contributes [vanilla]({{< relref "/flavours/vanilla" >}}) and a soft sweetness beneath the smoke.

For how peating actually works, see
[what makes a whisky peaty]({{< relref "/guides/what-makes-a-whisky-peaty" >}}).

## Core bottling

[Ardbeg 10 Year Old]({{< relref "/whiskies/ardbeg-10-year-old" >}}) is the reference
expression, bottled at 46% and without chill filtration.
