On two miles of the south coast of [Islay]({{< relref "/regions/islay" >}}), three
distilleries stand within sight of the same water. Ardbeg is the easternmost of them, a
short walk from [Lagavulin]({{< relref "/distilleries/lagavulin" >}}). It is owned by
**LVMH**, through Moët Hennessy, which acquired it via Glenmorangie in 2004.

**A note on the founding date.** Ardbeg is conventionally dated to **1815**, the year the
site was licensed as a commercial distillery. Wikidata records **1794**, reflecting
documented distilling on the site before any licence existed. Both dates are defensible.
They answer different questions.

## What makes it taste like that

Ardbeg specifies its malt at around **55 ppm** phenols, among the highest of any regularly
produced Scotch. On that number alone, one would predict a brutal whisky.

It is not one.

The moderating influence sits on the spirit still. A **purifier** catches the heaviest
vapours on their way out and turns them back for another passage through the copper. The
oilier compounds never complete the journey. What emerges is cleaner, and more precisely
drawn. The peat still arrives — but it arrives carrying citrus and sea air, rather than the
weight that [Lagavulin]({{< relref "/distilleries/lagavulin" >}}) brings.

The fill policy reinforces it. Ardbeg leans heavily on ex-bourbon American oak rather than
[sherry]({{< relref "/flavours/sherry" >}}), and that wood contributes
[vanilla]({{< relref "/flavours/vanilla" >}}) and a soft sweetness beneath the smoke instead
of dried fruit on top of it.

For how peating works at all, see
[what makes a whisky peaty]({{< relref "/guides/what-makes-a-whisky-peaty" >}}).

## Core bottling

[Ardbeg 10 Year Old]({{< relref "/whiskies/ardbeg-10-year-old" >}}) is the reference
expression: ten years, 46%, and no filter.
