Peated whisky is made from the same three permitted ingredients as unpeated whisky: water,
malted barley and yeast. The difference is introduced in a single step, early, and
everything downstream only modulates it.

## The mechanism

Harvested barley is steeped and allowed to germinate, converting starch into fermentable
sugar. Germination must then be halted by drying the grain.

If peat is burned under the drying floor, its smoke passes up through the damp malt.
Phenolic compounds in that smoke bind to the moist grain — and moisture is essential, which
is why peating happens at the start of drying, while the barley is still wet.

Those compounds then survive everything that follows: mashing, fermentation, distillation,
and years in oak. **Peat cannot be added later.** A whisky is either made from peated malt
or it is not.

## Why coastal peat tastes medicinal

Peat is partially decomposed vegetation, and different bogs decomposed different plants.

Inland Highland bogs formed from heather, grasses and woody material, giving a smoke closer
to a garden bonfire. Coastal [Islay]({{< relref "/regions/islay" >}}) bogs formed from
sphagnum moss and salt-laden, seaweed-rich vegetation, producing a phenol profile that reads
as [iodine]({{< relref "/flavours/iodine" >}}), antiseptic and tar.

[Yoichi]({{< relref "/distilleries/yoichi" >}}) in Hokkaido, using Japanese peat, lands
somewhere else again — drier and more mineral.

## Why the ppm number misleads

Boxes quote [phenol ppm]({{< relref "/glossary/phenol-ppm" >}}), and drinkers reasonably
read it as a smokiness rating. It is not.

The figure is measured **in the malt**, before distillation. Most of it never reaches the
bottle, and how much survives depends on the distillery's cut points, still shape, copper
contact and maturation.

The clearest demonstration sits two miles apart on Islay's south coast:

| | [Ardbeg]({{< relref "/distilleries/ardbeg" >}}) | [Lagavulin]({{< relref "/distilleries/lagavulin" >}}) |
|---|---|---|
| Malt specification | ~55 ppm | ~35 ppm |
| Still regime | Purifier, cleaner spirit | Very slow distillation |
| Cask emphasis | Ex-bourbon | Significant [sherry]({{< relref "/flavours/sherry" >}}) |
| Perceived weight | Bright, sharp, citric | Dense, sweet, weighty |

The more heavily peated malt produces the lighter-tasting whisky.

## Where to start

For peat as **attack** — bright, ashy, immediate — try
[Ardbeg 10 Year Old]({{< relref "/whiskies/ardbeg-10-year-old" >}}).

For peat as **depth** — smoke integrated into sweetness by sixteen years in wood — try
[Lagavulin 16 Year Old]({{< relref "/whiskies/lagavulin-16-year-old" >}}).

For a non-Scottish reference point, [Yoichi Single Malt]({{< relref "/whiskies/yoichi-single-malt" >}}).
